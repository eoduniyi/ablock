\begin{abstract}
We study a two-dimensional Clifford rotor-dilator update for finite-time particle exploration on synthetic multi-well landscapes. A neural potential is trained with denoising score matching (DSM), and particles are updated using $(\sigma I-\kappa)\nabla V_\theta$. We state sufficient Fokker--Planck conditions for Boltzmann stationarity, but the position-dependent $\sigma$ and $\kappa$ outputs used in our experiments are not constrained to satisfy them. On a synthetic three-basin benchmark, A-Block has lower terminal histogram free-energy-surface (FES) RMSE on a selected low-energy mask than standard Langevin ($1.059\pm0.031$ vs.\ $1.476\pm0.069$ kcal/mol), while standard Langevin has lower sliced Wasserstein distance and terminal B/C occupancy. A-Block records more transitions than the matched dilator-only ablation ($22.48\pm0.15$ vs.\ $13.86\pm0.15$ per particle), but only a small increase over standard Langevin ($21.47\pm0.13$). These finite-time results do not establish improved equilibrium sampling. Both A-Block and standard Langevin use $O(N)$ independent field evaluations; this work does not establish a runtime advantage or validate a molecular system.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Characterizing complex free-energy landscapes, predicting metastable transition paths, and generating diverse biomolecular conformations are central objectives of molecular machine learning~\citep{noe2019boltzmann, hoogeboom2022equivariant, du2022molgen}. While score-based generative modeling and geometric deep learning have enabled rapid structural synthesis~\citep{bronstein2021geometric, song2020score}, sampling from rugged multi-well distributions remains computationally challenging. In physical systems with high free-energy barriers between metastable states—such as multi-state signaling proteins undergoing conformational interchange~\citep{devore2016single}—reversible overdamped Langevin samplers frequently suffer from kinetic trapping or poor saddle-region resolution.

Mathematical analyses show that suitable non-reversible perturbations can improve mixing in some settings~\citep{hwang2005accelerating, lelievre2013optimal, duncan2016variance}. A divergence-free drift orthogonal to the potential gradient can preserve a target density under appropriate conditions; its effect on finite-time transitions depends on the drift, noise, and landscape. This motivates testing a transverse rotor with a score-based neural field, without assuming that it necessarily improves barrier crossing.

We introduce \textbf{A-Block} (Algebraic Block), a two-dimensional Clifford rotor-dilator update evaluated on a synthetic multi-well landscape and a glyph-navigation task. The rotor moves tangentially along level sets, while the dilator contracts along the potential gradient. We analyze sufficient conditions for stationary Boltzmann sampling and separately report finite-time results when those conditions are not enforced. Our contributions are: (1) a DSM scalar-potential model coupled to a $\operatorname{Cl}(2,0)$ update; (2) a Fokker--Planck analysis identifying conditions under which the modified drift preserves a target density; and (3) a synthetic benchmark with component ablations and distributional, occupancy, and transition metrics. The benchmark uses independent particle updates, so the $O(N)$ field-evaluation cost applies equally to standard Langevin; the rotor-dilator update adds a constant-factor operation cost. We do not present an empirical scaling study or a molecular validation.\footnote{Code and reproduction instructions: \url{https://github.com/eoduniyi/ablock}}

\section{The A-Block Architecture: Clifford Neural Score Matching}

An \textit{A-Block} models a modular ensemble of $N$ particle states $\mathbf{x}_i \in \Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ evolving in a bounded domain under a learned scalar field. Our multi-well benchmark is a synthetic two-dimensional potential, not a molecular simulation of a $(\phi,\psi)$ dihedral pair. The separate glyph task uses a potential constructed directly from a rasterized letterform.

\subsection{Neural Potential Parameterization, Energy Calibration, and DSM Training}

Let $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^2$ denote internal reaction coordinates governed by a multi-well potential $V(\mathbf{x})$ (in $\text{kcal/mol}$) at thermal energy $k_B T = 0.6\text{ kcal/mol}$. We model the target Boltzmann density $p(\mathbf{x}) \propto \exp(-V(\mathbf{x}) / k_B T)$ via a neural network (\texttt{CliffordScoreNet}) parameterizing a scalar potential $V_\theta(\mathbf{x}): \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ alongside rotor-dilator control channels $(\sigma(\mathbf{x}), \kappa(\mathbf{x}))$.

\textbf{Network Architecture and Training Protocol.} \texttt{CliffordScoreNet} is a 4-layer multilayer perceptron ($\mathbb{R}^2 \to 64 \to 64 \to 64 \to \mathbb{R}^3$) with $\operatorname{SiLU}$ activations. We initialize $12{,}000$ coordinates ($4{,}000$ per basin with Gaussian dispersion $0.35\text{--}0.40$) and apply $150$ unadjusted Langevin relaxation steps ($dt = 0.012, k_B T = 0.6$); this finite corpus is not asserted to be an equilibrated sample. The network is trained for $250$ epochs via Vincent--Song Denoising Score Matching (DSM)~\citep{song2020score} using $\operatorname{AdamW}$ ($\text{lr} = 2 \times 10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-5}$, cosine annealing, gradient clipping at $1.0$) over multi-scale noise perturbations $\sigma_n \in \{0.05, 0.10, 0.20\}$:
\vspace{-1mm}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{DSM}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x} \sim p_{\text{data}},\, \sigma_n,\, \boldsymbol{\epsilon} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})}\Bigl[ \sigma_n^2 \bigl\| -\nabla_{\mathbf{\tilde{x}}} V_\theta(\mathbf{\tilde{x}}) - \nabla_{\mathbf{\tilde{x}}} \log q_{\sigma_n}(\mathbf{\tilde{x}}|\mathbf{x}) \bigr\|^2 \Bigr],
\label{eq:score_matching}
\end{equation}
\vspace{-1mm}
where $\mathbf{\tilde{x}} = \mathbf{x} + \sigma_n \boldsymbol{\epsilon}$ and $\nabla_{\mathbf{\tilde{x}}} \log q_{\sigma_n}(\mathbf{\tilde{x}}|\mathbf{x}) = -\boldsymbol{\epsilon}/\sigma_n$. Equivalently, if $\boldsymbol{\eta}=\sigma_n\boldsymbol{\epsilon}$ is the scaled noise, the target is $-\boldsymbol{\eta}/\sigma_n^2$. The scalar output is trained to estimate the score of the generated corpus; its empirical scale factor $\alpha_E \approx 1.678\text{ (kcal/mol)}^{-1}$ is not an exact calibration guarantee.

\subsection{Geometric Algebra Formulation of Rotor-Dilator Dynamics ($\operatorname{Cl}(2,0)$)}

Standard score-guided Langevin dynamics evolve strictly along $-\nabla V_\theta(\mathbf{x})$. To introduce non-reversible circulation along equipotential contours, we work in the 2D Clifford Geometric Algebra $\operatorname{Cl}(2,0)$ with orthonormal basis $\{e_1, e_2\}$ and unit bivector pseudoscalar $I = e_1 e_2$ ($I^2 = -1$). While a monolithic $D$-dimensional Clifford algebra $\operatorname{Cl}(D,0)$ incurs $2^D$ basis growth, macromolecular backbones with $K$ dihedral hinges $(\phi_k, \psi_k)$ decompose into a direct sum of planar subalgebras $\bigoplus_{k=1}^K \operatorname{Cl}(2,0)$, scaling linearly as $4K$ rather than $2^{2K}$.

We define the multivector drift operator as a coupled \textit{rotor-dilator}:
\vspace{-1mm}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{f}_i(\mathbf{x}_i) = (\sigma_i I - \kappa_i) \nabla V_\theta(\mathbf{x}_i),
\label{eq:clifford_flow}
\end{equation}
\vspace{-1mm}
where $I \nabla V_\theta = (-\partial_{y} V_\theta, \partial_{x} V_\theta)^\top$ rotates the gradient by $90^\circ$, $\sigma_i = 1.5 \tanh(\cdot) \in [-1.5, 1.5]$ controls rotor chirality, and $\kappa_i = \operatorname{softplus}(\cdot) + 0.1$ sets the dilator rate. The DSM loss depends only on $V_\theta$: the $\sigma$ and $\kappa$ output heads receive no direct gradients from that objective. Their reported values ($\bar{\sigma}=-1.31\pm0.26$, $\bar{\kappa}=0.33\pm0.17$) therefore describe unsupervised readouts, not learned optimal controls or a demonstrated thermodynamic calibration.

\textbf{Overdamped Barrier-Crossing Mechanism.} Each particle evolves via the overdamped It\^o stochastic differential equation (SDE):
\vspace{-1mm}
\begin{equation}
\gamma d\mathbf{x}_i = (\sigma_i I - \kappa_i) \nabla V_\theta(\mathbf{x}_i) dt + \sqrt{2 \gamma k_B T} d\mathbf{W}_i(t).
\label{eq:langevin}
\end{equation}
\vspace{-1mm}
Because Eq.~(\ref{eq:langevin}) is overdamped, particles carry no inertial momentum, and pure deterministic rotation $\sigma I \nabla V_\theta$ preserves potential energy ($\nabla V_\theta \cdot I \nabla V_\theta = 0$). The rotor therefore moves tangentially along equipotential level sets. Depending on the field geometry and coefficient, this motion can carry particles toward or away from saddle regions; thermal noise supports transitions, but the identity alone does not imply faster barrier crossing.

\subsection{Stationarity Conditions and Non-Equilibrium Currents}

A fundamental question raised by modifying the drift in Eq.~(\ref{eq:langevin}) is how $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ and $\kappa(\mathbf{x})$ affect the stationary distribution $\rho_\infty(\mathbf{x})$ of the Fokker--Planck equation $\partial_t \rho = -\nabla \cdot \mathbf{J}(\mathbf{x}, t)$.

\begin{proposition}[Sufficient Fokker--Planck Stationarity Condition]
\label{prop:stationarity}
Consider the overdamped SDE $d\mathbf{x} = \gamma^{-1}(\sigma(\mathbf{x}) I - \kappa(\mathbf{x}))\nabla V_\theta(\mathbf{x}) dt + \sqrt{2 D}\, d\mathbf{W}_t$ with diffusion $D = k_B T / \gamma$. The probability flux decomposes into reversible and circulatory currents $\mathbf{J} = \mathbf{J}_{\text{rev}} + \mathbf{J}_{\text{irr}}$:
\vspace{-1mm}
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} = \nabla \cdot \Bigl(\underbrace{\gamma^{-1}\kappa(\mathbf{x}) \rho \nabla V_\theta + D \nabla \rho}_{-\mathbf{J}_{\text{rev}}}\Bigr) - \nabla \cdot \Bigl(\underbrace{\gamma^{-1}\sigma(\mathbf{x}) I \nabla V_\theta \, \rho}_{\mathbf{J}_{\text{irr}}}\Bigr).
\end{equation}
\vspace{-1mm}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Measure-Preserving Non-Reversible Regime:} If $\kappa$ is constant (with calibrated potential $\kappa V_\theta = V$, i.e., $\kappa = \alpha_E^{-1} = k_B T$), $\sigma(\mathbf{x}) = \sigma(V_\theta(\mathbf{x}))$ depends only on the potential level, and the domain has suitable no-flux or vanishing-at-infinity boundary conditions, then $\rho_{\text{B}}(\mathbf{x}) = Z^{-1}\exp(-\kappa V_\theta(\mathbf{x})/k_B T) = Z^{-1}\exp(-V(\mathbf{x})/k_B T)$ is stationary. Specifically, $\mathbf{J}_{\text{rev}}(\rho_{\text{B}}) = \mathbf{0}$ and, because $\nabla \cdot (I \nabla V_\theta) = -\partial_x\partial_y V_\theta + \partial_y\partial_x V_\theta = 0$ and $\nabla \sigma(V_\theta) = \sigma'(V_\theta)\nabla V_\theta \perp I \nabla V_\theta$, the circulatory current is divergence-free:
    \vspace{-1mm}
    \begin{equation}
    \nabla \cdot \mathbf{J}_{\text{irr}}(\rho_{\text{B}}) = \gamma^{-1}\bigl(\nabla \sigma(V_\theta) \cdot I \nabla V_\theta\bigr)\rho_{\text{B}} + \gamma^{-1}\sigma(V_\theta) \bigl(\nabla \rho_{\text{B}} \cdot I \nabla V_\theta\bigr) = 0.
    \end{equation}
    \vspace{-1mm}
    \item \textbf{Position-dependent coefficients:} If the sufficient conditions above fail (for example, if $\nabla \sigma(\mathbf{x}) \cdot I \nabla V_\theta \neq 0$), the target Boltzmann density need not be stationary. A non-Boltzmann stationary density with nonzero probability current may arise under suitable confinement and boundary conditions; this condition alone does not establish its existence or properties.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

In 2D, the Clifford bivector $I=e_1e_2$ is the coordinate-free generator of a skew-symmetric drift, connecting this formulation to non-reversible Langevin methods~\citep{hwang2005accelerating, lelievre2013optimal, duncan2016variance}. The result is conditional: in the benchmark, the position-dependent $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ and $\kappa(\mathbf{x})$ are not constrained to satisfy the sufficient conditions, and we do not claim that the simulated A-Block process has the target Boltzmann stationary distribution. The comparisons below are finite-time comparisons using the same learned scalar field.

\section{Per-Step Complexity and Scope}

We represent the particle ensemble by its empirical measure
\vspace{-1mm}
\begin{equation}
\mu_t = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \delta_{\mathbf{x}_i(t)}.
\end{equation}
\vspace{-1mm}
\textbf{Independent-particle cost.} In the experiments, particles are updated independently in a shared field. Evaluating that field for $N$ particles costs $O(N)$ per step for both standard Langevin and A-Block; the rotor-dilator combination adds constant-factor arithmetic. This is not a complexity advantage over Langevin. An explicit all-pairs interaction graph would require $O(N^2)$ distance checks, but no such graph baseline is part of our experiments. We report no empirical scaling curve or wall-clock time-to-accuracy comparison. Extending the update to $K$ independent two-dimensional coordinates would cost $O(NK)$ under that independence assumption; this does not establish complexity for coupled molecular degrees of freedom.

\begin{proposition}[Cost of independent field evaluation]
\label{prop:complexity}
For fixed network architecture and coordinate dimension, evaluating the shared field independently at $N$ particle states costs $O(N)$ for either standard Langevin or the A-Block update. The asymptotic order is unchanged by the rotor-dilator arithmetic.
\end{proposition}

\section{Empirical Evaluation and Ablation Benchmark}

We evaluated A-Block on a canonical 2D triple-well conformational landscape featuring three metastable basins: Basin A at $(-1.0, 1.0)$ ($d_A=3.5, w_A=0.55$), Basin B at $(1.0, -0.5)$ ($d_B=4.0, w_B=0.60$), and Basin C at $(-0.5, -1.0)$ ($d_C=3.2, w_C=0.50$), with harmonic confinement $0.20\|\mathbf{x}\|^2$ (all energies in $\text{kcal/mol}$). All samplers were initialized with $N = 2{,}000$ particles localized inside Basin A ($\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{c}_A, 0.15^2 \mathbf{I})$) and integrated for $T = 1{,}000$ steps ($dt = 0.015, \gamma = 1.0, k_B T = 0.6$).

\textbf{Evaluated Baselines and Ablations.} To isolate each architectural component (specifically addressing whether FES gains arise from contraction $\kappa$ or rotor circulation $\sigma I$), we benchmarked five configurations across 5 random seeds: (1)~\textbf{Brownian Dynamics ($\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{0}$):} unconditional diffusion; (2)~\textbf{Standard Langevin ($\mathbf{f}=-\nabla V_\theta$):} unit-contraction gradient drift ($\kappa=1, \sigma=0$); (3)~\textbf{Dilator-Only Ablation ($\mathbf{f}=-\kappa\nabla V_\theta$):} matched contraction using the network's $\kappa_i$ head with zero rotation ($\sigma=0$); (4)~\textbf{Rotor-Only Ablation ($\mathbf{f}=\sigma I\nabla V_\theta$):} pure contour circulation without contractive restoration ($\kappa=0$); and (5)~\textbf{A-Block Full Clifford ($\mathbf{f}=(\sigma I - \kappa)\nabla V_\theta$):} coupled rotor-dilator dynamics.

\textbf{Formal Metric Definitions.} Let $\{\mathbf{x}_i(t)\}_{i=1}^N$ denote particle trajectories and $\{\mathbf{y}_j\}_{j=1}^N \sim p_{\text{B}}(\mathbf{x}) \propto e^{-V(\mathbf{x})/k_B T}$ denote exact Boltzmann reference samples on $[-2.2, 2.2]^2$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Free Energy Surface (FES) RMSE ($\text{kcal/mol}$):} From the terminal 2D histogram $p_{\text{emp}}$ ($35\times 35$ bins on $[-2.2, 2.2]^2$), we estimate $F_{\text{est}}(\mathbf{x}) = -k_B T \ln(p_{\text{emp}}(\mathbf{x}) + 10^{-5})$, align the minimum offset across the low-energy mask $\mathcal{M} = \{\mathbf{x} : V(\mathbf{x}) < -1.2\text{ kcal/mol}\}$, and compute $\bigl(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}|}\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{M}} (F_{\text{est}}(\mathbf{x}) - V(\mathbf{x}))^2\bigr)^{1/2}$.
    \item \textbf{Sliced 1-Wasserstein Distance ($\mathcal{W}_1$):} Average 1D Earth-Mover's distance across $M=64$ unit projections $\boldsymbol{\theta}_m \in \mathbb{S}^1$: $\mathcal{W}_1 = \frac{1}{64N}\sum_{m=1}^{64}\sum_{i=1}^N |\boldsymbol{\theta}_m^\top \mathbf{x}_{(i)}(T) - \boldsymbol{\theta}_m^\top \mathbf{y}_{(i)}|$.
    \item \textbf{Terminal B/C Occupancy (\%), Cumulative Visitation (\%), and Transitions:} Define basin masks $\mathcal{B}_k = \{\mathbf{x} : \|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{c}_k\|^2 < 0.45\}$ for $k \in \{A, B, C\}$. Terminal B/C occupancy is the fraction of particles in $\mathcal{B}_B \cup \mathcal{B}_C$ at $t=T$; its exact grid-based Boltzmann reference is $63.6\%$. Cumulative visitation is the fraction of trajectories that enter either basin at any step, and transitions per particle count inter-basin state changes. Visitation and transition counts measure exploration, not agreement with the equilibrium distribution.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Analysis of Benchmark Results, Ablations, and Sensitivity Checks}

\textbf{Interpreting the mixed metrics.} A-Block has lower terminal histogram FES RMSE than standard Langevin on the selected mask, but standard Langevin has lower sliced $\mathcal{W}_1$ distance ($0.066$ vs.\ $0.385$) and terminal B/C occupancy ($60.9\%$ vs.\ $37.4\%$). The exact grid-based Boltzmann reference places $63.6\%$ of its mass in B/C, making Langevin's terminal occupancy closer to the reference. We therefore do not interpret the lower occupancy or higher $\mathcal{W}_1$ as evidence of physically desirable circulation. Cumulative B/C visitation is $99.7\%$ for both methods and measures whether particles reached the basins, not how accurately their terminal distribution matches equilibrium. The FES RMSE is a complementary local metric: it is computed only where $V<-1.2$ and aligns the minimum offset, so it does not override the distributional metrics. The matched-contraction ablation has FES RMSE $1.064\pm0.044$, close to A-Block's $1.059\pm0.031$; the clearest difference is the transition count ($13.86\pm0.15$ vs.\ $22.48\pm0.15$ per particle). A-Block's transition count is only modestly higher than standard Langevin's ($22.48\pm0.15$ vs.\ $21.47\pm0.13$), and we make no time-to-accuracy or sampling-efficiency claim.

\textbf{Timestep and histogram-bin sensitivity.} We reran Standard Langevin, Dilator-Only, and A-Block at $dt=0.005$, $T=3{,}000$ (same physical horizon), using seed 42; the complete metrics are in \texttt{output/timestep\_sensitivity.json}. A-Block has FES RMSE $1.041$ kcal/mol and $25.99$ transitions/particle, compared with $1.503$ and $25.73$ for Standard Langevin, and $1.058$ and $16.14$ for Dilator-Only. Thus the transition advantage over Dilator-Only persists in this diagnostic, while the transition counts for A-Block and Standard Langevin are similar. This is a single-seed sensitivity check, not a convergence or efficiency study. Across the five evaluation seeds, mean FES RMSE $\pm$ standard deviation at $25\times25$, $35\times35$, and $50\times50$ bins is $0.803\pm0.031$, $1.059\pm0.031$, and $1.303\pm0.029$ for A-Block, versus $1.312\pm0.032$, $1.476\pm0.069$, and $1.579\pm0.049$ for Standard Langevin. These estimates all use the same low-energy mask and minimum-offset alignment.

\section{Conclusion and Limitations}
We presented a two-dimensional Clifford rotor-dilator update and evaluated it on synthetic multi-well and glyph-derived potentials. The multi-well results are mixed: A-Block has lower FES RMSE on the selected mask and a modestly higher transition count than standard Langevin, while Langevin is closer in terminal B/C occupancy and sliced Wasserstein distance. The analysis gives sufficient conditions for Boltzmann stationarity, but the position-dependent control outputs used in the experiments are not constrained to meet them. The work does not establish a runtime or scaling advantage over Langevin, and neither benchmark is a molecular simulation. Further work should compare against established nonreversible samplers, assess convergence and time-to-accuracy, directly train or constrain the control outputs, and validate on a coupled molecular system.
